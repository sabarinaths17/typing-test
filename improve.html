How to Type Faster

A practical guide covering everything from finger placement and posture to building long-term speed and accuracy. Follow these principles consistently and you'll see measurable improvement within weeks.

01 — Master Touch Typing Foundation

Touch typing means typing without looking at the keyboard. It is the single most impactful skill you can build. Glancing at your keys breaks your flow, slows you down, and caps your ceiling — professional typists never look down.

Start slow. It will feel uncomfortable for the first week. That discomfort is your brain rewiring. Push through it.

  • Commit fully — resist the urge to look. Cover the keyboard or use a blank key set if needed.
  • Slow down to speed up — typing at 80% accuracy at 30 WPM is more valuable than hunting at 60 WPM with constant corrections.
  • Every key has an assigned finger. Stick to the assignment even when it feels awkward.

02 — Learn the Home Row Fundamentals

Your fingers rest on the home row — ASDF (left hand) and JKL; (right hand) — and return there after every keystroke. This is your anchor. The small bumps on F and J are your tactile guides.

03 — Posture & Ergonomics Setup

Poor posture leads to fatigue and eventually injury. Good posture lets you type longer without strain.

  • Sit upright with your back supported. Avoid hunching toward the screen.
  • Elbows at ~90° and wrists level with or slightly below your keyboard. Your wrists should float, not press against the desk.
  • Screen at eye level — use a stand if needed. Looking down all day strains your neck.
  • Feet flat on the floor. If your chair is too high, use a footrest.
  • Keyboard tilt — flat or slightly tilted away from you (negative tilt) is easiest on your wrists. The raised legs on the back of most keyboards are ergonomically backwards.
  • Take breaks. The 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Stretch your fingers and wrists regularly.

04 — Accuracy First, Speed Second Method

This is the most common mistake: chasing WPM before building accuracy. High error rates slow you down more than a slower, precise pace. Every correction costs you more time than the mistake saved.

Target 95%+ accuracy at your current speed before pushing for more WPM. If you're below 90%, you're practicing mistakes — slow down until accuracy climbs.

05 — Target Your Weak Keys Deliberate Practice

Typing speed is limited by your slowest keys and sequences. Random practice doesn't fix targeted weaknesses — deliberate practice does.

  • Identify which keys or bigrams slow you down. Common offenders: Q, Z, X, P, numbers, and common combos like "ion", "tion", "th".
  • Drill them specifically — type rows of words containing those letters until they feel smooth.
  • Words > random letters. Typing "question" ten times is more useful than typing "q" a hundred times — your brain learns in context.
  • Track your worst sequences in the analytics tab and prioritize them.

06 — Daily Practice Routine Consistency

Frequency beats duration. 15 minutes every day outperforms 2 hours once a week. Typing is a motor skill — it lives in muscle memory, which is built through repetition over time, not cramming.

A solid daily routine: 5 min warm-up (easy mode), 10 min hard focused practice, 5 min free typing on something you actually need to write.

  • Warm up slowly — don't start at full speed. Let your fingers wake up.
  • Use the Hard difficulty on TypeForge to practice technical vocabulary if you code.
  • Transcription practice — type out articles, book excerpts, or code you need to read anyway. Real text is more useful than random words.

07 — Habits to Break Common Pitfalls

  • Two-finger ("hunt and peck") typing — physically limits you to ~40 WPM. Touch typing is the only path to real speed.
  • Looking at the keyboard — breaks focus and flow every single time.
  • Chasing WPM immediately — leads to sloppy habits that are hard to unlearn.
  • Typing through pain. Wrist pain, finger pain, or forearm ache are signals to rest and check your posture.
  • Not returning to home row. If your fingers wander, accuracy collapses on the next word.
  • Inconsistent finger assignment. Always use the same finger for each key. Inconsistency means each keypress requires a tiny decision.
  • Skipping accuracy targets. If you can't hit 95% at 40 WPM, don't try to hit 95% at 60 WPM yet.
